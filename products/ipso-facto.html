Narrative Story

Everyone has loved someone through the things they left behind, and for me it was a t-shirt, oversized and worn thin at the collar, that he left on the bed. He was a rare creature — an athletic nerd who could shred on a bass and hold his own on a drum kit, quiet unless he had something to say — but I loved him completely. I would fall asleep on his leg, my head on his thigh, and the scent of his t-shirt — clean sweat, cotton, and something warm and woody and entirely him — would be the last thing I knew before sleep. When I cried, he would pull me under his arm, and the smell of that shirt was a fortress: comfort and security and reassurance, a promise without words. It smelled like being safe. It smelled like being chosen. He is gone, and the shirt proves he was there. I still have one, you know. It's washed a thousand times now, but if I close my eyes and press my face to the cotton, I can almost find him in it.

八、他的T恤

他的衣服从来不叠，扔在椅背上，穿的时候拿起来抖一抖。我趴在他腿上睡觉，脸贴着他的大腿，闻到的就是那件T恤：棉的，洗过很多次，软了，上面有他身上的味道，不是香水，是洗衣粉、一点汗、还有皮肤本身那种温温的、带点咸的气味。

我哭的时候他不说话，胳膊一收，把我整个人圈进去。T恤的布贴在我脸上，有点粗糙，但我把鼻子埋进去，闻着那个味道，就觉得什么事都能过去。他说“没事”，声音从胸口传过来，闷闷的，震着我的耳朵。

后来分开了。那件T恤我带走了。洗过很多次，早就没有他的味道了，但我有时候还是会把脸埋进去，不是闻，是找。找那个被圈住的、什么都不用怕的感觉。
